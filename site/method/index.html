Explicit vs implicit: how a time step is taken

An explicit step moves each node by the force it feels now; an implicit step solves for the positions at which forces balance at the end of the step, and Aleph takes every step implicitly.

  • Explicit

    • The force now gives the next position directly.
    • Cheap per step: one force evaluation, no solve.
    • Stable only while dt is below the fastest relaxation time in the world.
    • With stiff bonds and solvent drag that time is about 2 × 10⁻¹¹ s, so one millisecond needs about 5 × 10⁷ steps (1 ms ÷ 2 × 10⁻¹¹ s).

  • Implicit

    • Solves for the next state so that every node's forces balance at the end of the step.
    • Stable at a large dt: no relaxation time limits the step.
    • Each step is a nonlinear solve over the whole world.
    • The step counts only when it is solved at tol_F; otherwise it is labelled FORCE-ACCEPTED.

Why Aleph chose implicit

  • The bonds are stiff and the drag is weak

    With solvent-only drag the elastic relaxation time is about 2 × 10⁻¹¹ s, and explicit stepping blew up.More

    This is the STATE (a) row on the engine (aleph/physics).

  • A cell needs milliseconds and longer

    Motors, membrane flow and transport act over milliseconds to minutes.More

    At the explicit limit a millisecond is about 5 × 10⁷ steps; at the 10⁻⁶ s live point it is 1,000 steps.

  • Exactness is checked at the end of every step

    The time architecture has an outer loop in physical time and an inner mechanical solve that stops at tol_F.FORCE-ACCEPTEDMore

    The rule SHORTCUT-EXACT judges a step only by its end-point residual, so any route to that end point is allowed (coupled solves, preconditioners, predictions), and a step whose residual stays above tol_F is labelled FORCE-ACCEPTED, never reported as solved.

What implicit forces us to build

New mechanisms are implemented; each is going through testing; any that shows no advantage will be dropped.

  • Newton solver with line search and a trust region in use

    ProjectedNewton with a line search solves each step; the trust region and the rigid retraction (rotations stay rotations) are going through testing.

  • Linear solves and preconditioners in use

    Each Newton direction is a CG or MINRES solve.More

    The first plain Jacobi try at dt 10⁻⁵ s had κ ≈ 10⁵ and was not solved in 50 iterations, which is why block-Jacobi came next; ChainBand and SACoarse are going through testing.

  • Contact inside the solve in use

    Contact is part of the same world energy, and a CCD bound shortens any stride that would let two surfaces pass through each other.

  • A stop rule and honest FORCE-ACCEPTED labels in use

    F ≤ min(tol_F, 0.1 × F0), signed 7 October; a step that reaches the iteration cap above that is kept and labelled FORCE-ACCEPTED.

  • The cost per step not yet

    A native step at the 10⁻⁶ s live point read 51.168 s (S123m, 14 Sep), most of it the thermal draw inside the mechanical work; this is the main open cost.

  • Warm starts and the NN fast track in testing

    A learned starting guess for Newton is implemented and going through testing; no measured speed-up yet.More

    The final iterations still check tol_F.

  • Multi-GPU row split in testing

    RowSplitNewton splits one world's rows across 2 cards and solves the same equation; it is going through testing.

Open problems

  • Steps that hit the cap

    Some steps reach the iteration cap above tol_F and are FORCE-ACCEPTED; the census below counts how many worlds have every step solved at tol_F.

  • Slow modes in the linear solve

    MINRES stalls on slow coupled modes traced to the closed surfaces' flux rows.More

    A multilevel preconditioner for this was implemented (MINRESPC handback, 16 Oct) and was slower on a reduced world, so the slow mode remains.

  • The dt walls at 10⁻⁵ and 10⁻⁴ s

    At 10⁻⁵ s the strands now hold but the membrane folds or its area is not solved (dt-surfaces, 10 and 11 Sep); at 10⁻⁴ s the strands fail by step 10 and the surfaces by step 12 (P7d, 11 Sep).

What “converged” means here

Convergence is a word with several meanings.More

Aleph keeps them apart: a solved step, a run whose steps were solved, a model that matches physics, and a result that does not change when the time step or grid is refined. Each is a separate claim with its own evidence.

  1. One step is solved when the leftover force is small

    Each time step is implicit: the engine looks for node positions at which every node's forces balance, including the drag and inertia of that step.More

    What it cannot balance is the residual F, the largest net force left on any single node, in piconewtons (pN). The step is solved when F ≤ tol_F. The residual is a backward error: the extra force under which the final positions would solve the step exactly. It is not a bound on how far any node is from its exact position.

  2. The scale of tol_F

    tol_F = k_BT / Δx, the thermal energy divided by the node spacing.More

    At 310 K, k_BT = 4.28 × 10⁻³ pN·µm, and with Δx = 0.05 µm this gives tol_F ≈ 0.086 pN. A world run at T = 0 keeps the same declared value.

  3. The relative rule (signed 7 October 2026)

    A step must also reduce its own starting force tenfold: F ≤ min(tol_F, 0.1 × F0), where F0 is the residual when the step begins.More

    Before this rule, a step that started just below tol_F counted as solved after 0 Newton iterations; in a test chain that left an error of 0.11 of the amplitude against the exact step; on the same chain and seeds under the relative rule, 0.024. When F0 is already at the level of float64 round-off, the bound is raised to that step's own recorded round-off floor (signed 8 October), computed from the step's numbers with nothing tuned.

  4. Per-node budgets in the development worlds

    The development worlds counted below ran a declared variant of the same rule.More

    Each node's leftover force is divided by that node's own budget (one tenth of the force scale of its thermal kick or, when it has no thermal scale, the force that would move it 0.1 Δx against its drag in one step). The step is solved when the largest ratio r ≤ 1, with the same tenfold relative tightening. The chart below plots r.

  5. FORCE-ACCEPTED

    If the solver reaches its iteration cap with the residual still above tol_F, the step is kept and labelled FORCE-ACCEPTED.force-acceptedMore

    Nothing in the physics accepts or rejects a step. The label means “what the solver reached in N iterations” and is reported with F and N. A force-accepted step is never called a solved step, an equilibrium or a relaxed state, and a run with any force-accepted step is cited as force-accepted.

  6. A run is classed by the fraction of its steps at tol_F

    Run census classes: all steps reached tol_F; tol_F reached but the run is short (two steps or fewer) or was stopped by its wall-clock limit; partly (some steps reached tol_F); failed or none.More

    In the census read on 16 October at 11:33, 25 development worlds by their best arm were 17 / 5 / 0 / 3 in these four classes, and by their latest record 11 / 3 / 2 / 9.

  7. Physical verification is a separate question

    A solved step means the step's equations were solved.More

    Whether those equations describe the physics is checked against a closed-form prediction declared before the run and read from the stored frames. Two development examples: a vesicle's l = 2 shape mode relaxing in a Stokes solvent, compared with Milner–Safran (engine/theory rate 0.952); and the M3 motor rig against its mean-field slope (0.751 ± 0.033 µm/s against 0.750 with no load, 0.366 ± 0.027 against 0.375 at half-stall; ± is 3 SE, one seed). Both are cell-free development worlds, not full-size records.

  8. Time-step and grid convergence are different again

    Solver convergence is about one step at a fixed time step and node spacing.force-acceptedMore

    Time-step convergence asks whether an observable agrees at dt, dt/2 and dt/4 within a band declared beforehand; grid convergence asks the same under a finer node spacing or fluid grid. Only solved steps may enter such a study, because force-accepted steps mix solver error into discretisation error. No time-step or grid convergence study on solved steps is shown on this site yet.

What does not count as evidence

  • A force-accepted trajectory is not a physical trajectory. It is what the solver reached in the iterations it was given.
  • A surrogate, reduced or compressed result is not a reference result. Its approximation error is reported with it.
  • A score must come from a measurement declared before the run and read from the stored record, never from a number the run chose to keep.
  • A parameter that the observation cannot see is not reported as a single value.
  • A law shown on one cell type is not universal until it is shown to transfer to another.
  • A model is not scored against answers its own machinery produced.
  • A cell-level run without the fluid is a labelled approximation, never a conclusion.
  • Passing code tests is not a production result, and a world below the full native size supports no physics conclusion.
  • Nothing is called quantum unless quantum dynamics were computed.

How the residual is reduced in one step

The section above says when a step counts as solved.More

This one says how the engine gets there: one operator for the whole world, a Newton direction from that operator, a preconditioned linear solve, a safe stride bounded by contact, and the stop. The narration of each beat is the same text the film uses; the engine's class and kernel names are under each beat.

  1. 1. One equation per step

    Every node of the cell (filament bead, membrane vertex, crosslinker) has mass and feels drag.More

    To move the world forward by one time step, the engine writes one energy for the whole world: the cost of moving against drag and inertia plus every relation's energy. The new positions are where this energy is lowest, which means the forces on every node balance.

    Engine names

    Unknowns: every node position at t + dt. Π(x) = ½ Σ D_i |x_i − x̂_i|² + Σ E(x), with D_i = γ_i/dt + m_i/dt² and γ_i = 6πηR_i. Setting ∇Π = 0 is implicit (backward-Euler) force balance: drag + inertia + every relation force = 0.

  2. 2. Relations become forces

    The engine reads each relation table (segments, bending triples, membrane faces, hinges, contacts) and gives each row two jobs.More

    It reports its force on its nodes, and it reports how that force changes if the nodes move. No part of the cell gets a solver of its own. All of it is summed into one world operator. Motors are not a force inside this solve: they act between two solves, by shifting a rest length or switching a partner at the solved positions, and the next solve balances the new relations.

    Engine names

    world_terms maps segments → PairTerm, bending triples → BendingTerm, faces → FaceTerm, hinges → HingeTerm; contact adds SdfTerm and BarrierTerm, the fluid adds StokesCoupling, the area law adds FaceAreaALM. Every term has three launchers: gather (its force on each node plus that node's own 3 × 3 diagonal block), hvp (a Hessian–vector product from element-wise positive semidefinite Hessians) and energy. The full Hessian is never stored; only the node-diagonal blocks are. Motors: between_solves.

  3. 3. The residual

    At any trial position, the engine adds up the net force on each node.More

    A node at balance has net force zero. The largest leftover force is the residual. A step is solved at tol_F only when that leftover is small for every node: below one thermal energy divided by the node spacing, below a tenth of the step's own thermal kick, and below a tenth of the force the step started with.

    Engine names

    _finish_grad_kernel: g_i = ∇E_i + D_i(x_i − x̂_i); F = max |g_i|. tol_F = 1.0 × k_BT/Δx. Stop: F ≤ max(min(tol_F, 0.1 F0), floor), where the floor is the step's own float64 round-off level. Per-node budget: r_i = |g_i| / (0.1 s_i), s_i = √(2k_BT γ_i/dt), and r = max r_i ≤ 1. These are backward-error rules.

  4. 4. Newton's direction

    From the current guess, the engine asks: if the forces were linear, which move would cancel them all?More

    It answers with conjugate gradients on the GPU. Each filament is solved exactly along its own length, and patches of membrane are corrected as coarse rigid pieces. Iterations still in progress stay loose; the last ones are tight.

    Engine names

    Linear system (D + H)p = −g, symmetric positive definite, so conjugate gradients (_cg) with Hessian products from _hessvec. Preconditioner (_precondition): node block-Jacobi + ChainBand (an exact banded LDLᵀ factor per filament chain) + SACoarse (smoothed aggregation on six rigid modes per patch). Eisenstat–Walker forcing (_forcing_kernel): CG stops at ‖r‖ ≤ η_k ‖r₀‖ or 400 iterations.

  5. 5. A safe stride

    The proposed move is checked before it is taken.More

    Contacts set the longest stride that keeps any two surfaces from passing through each other. The engine then halves the stride until the total energy has fallen enough. Filaments bend and rotate along their own arc instead of stretching in straight lines.

    Engine names

    Contact rows (point–triangle and edge–edge barrier pairs, confinement to surfaces) are ordinary terms of Π and also bound the stride: SdfTerm and BarrierBound (continuous collision detection) lower α_max. _line_search on the arc path tries α_max × {1, ½, …} (16 lengths) with the Armijo test c1 = 10⁻⁴ on the per-term energy change (_armijo_chunk_kernel → _armijo_kernel). If no length passes, the block-Jacobi direction is tried (_jacobi_dir_kernel); if that also fails, the step ends stalled.

  6. 6. Fluid and heat in the same step

    The surrounding fluid is solved with Stokes flow, and its drag enters through triangle faces of the membrane.More

    Newer mechanisms sit beside this path: other fluid couplings, rigid six-degree-of-freedom bodies and a trust-region step. All of these are implemented. Each is now going through testing, and any that shows no advantage will be dropped. Thermal kicks are drawn once per step and held fixed while the solver works.

    Engine names

    StokesCoupling.begin_step solves Stokes on a staggered (MAC) grid (Uzawa–Richardson with a multigrid V-cycle) and couples through Peskin 4-point spreading and interpolation; each face adds a drag term to Π. ProjectedNewton.begin draws ξ_i = √(2k_BT γ_i/dt) · N(0, I) and moves the anchor x̂_i by ξ_i/D_i.

  7. 7. The stop, and honesty

    The loop repeats (residual, direction, stride) until the residual falls below the bar.FORCE-ACCEPTEDMore

    A step that reaches the bar is recorded as solved at tol_F. A step that runs out of iterations is kept, but it is labelled FORCE-ACCEPTED: what the solver reached, not a balanced state. Every node's position and force is saved losslessly either way.

    Engine names

    _check_kernel and _tcheck_kernel write the decision to a control word on the GPU; there is no host read inside the loop. Newton cap: 300 iterations. _record_kernel keeps each iteration's Π, gᵀp, α and CG count.

Mechanisms implemented beside this path

Besides the conjugate-gradient path above, the engine holds other ways to reach the same stop.More

All of these are implemented. Each is now going through testing, and any that shows no advantage will be dropped. The fluid can be solved inside the same Newton system: one MINRES solve per iteration on the saddle of node moves, solvent and pressure (MonolithicStep), or on a fluid mesh fitted to the nodes, in which rigid bodies are holes with no grid inside. A trust-region step (TrustRegionStep: Steihaug–Toint conjugate gradients on the exact element Hessians, with a region that grows or shrinks by the ratio of actual to predicted energy decrease) can replace the line search. Rigid six-degree-of-freedom bodies (RigidBodies) keep every node's mass and forces, but their motion is held to x = c + R y; the linear solve runs on each body's six modes and the line search follows the exact rigid path, a rigid retraction. Exact filament length constraints (LengthKKT) and a solve split across two GPUs (RowSplitNewton) are further mechanisms.

The other simulators

These are the tools the project's literature base describes in enough detail to profile. Each profile uses only that base's audited sources (verdict OK); tools it does not describe are not profiled.

  • Cytosim

    Cytosim represents fibres as strings of points in a viscous medium with fixed lengths and Brownian motion. [1]

    More

    It was built to simulate fibres, spheres and solids in a viscous medium where inertia is negligible. Fibres keep their length by constraints and resist bending; motion follows a first-order Langevin equation. “Fibers, spheres and other voluminous objects are represented with points.”

  • AFINES

    AFINES builds a filament from beads joined by harmonic stretch and bend potentials, and ignores hydrodynamic interactions between beads. [2]

    More

    A coarse-grained two-dimensional network of actin, myosin minifilaments and crosslinkers, at long time and length scales: “We model actin filaments as polar wormlike chains (WLCs).”

  • MEDYAN

    MEDYAN represents filaments as cylinders and couples stochastic chemistry to mechanics, with myosin mini-filaments and alpha-actinin crosslinkers. [3]

    More

    Spatially resolved stochastic reaction–diffusion is coupled to a cylinder-based mechanical model, a “polymer model based on elongated cylindrical monomer segments for simulating semi-flexible polymers”. After a number of reaction steps the network is brought to mechanical equilibrium by energy minimisation.

  • Cytosim, AFINES and MEDYAN together

    Cytosim, AFINES and MEDYAN are agent-based cytoskeleton tools: Cytosim and AFINES integrate Langevin dynamics, while MEDYAN minimises the mechanical energy after its reaction steps. [4] [1] [2] [3]

  • PhysiCell, CompuCell3D and vertex models

    PhysiCell, CompuCell3D and vertex models work at the scale of whole cells and tissues, not individual filaments. [5] [6] [7] [8] [9]

    More

    PhysiCell: “able to simulate large numbers of cells in high throughput”, with cells as agents in a continuum microenvironment. CompuCell3D is a cellular Potts platform; a cellular Potts model is “a cell-level modeling approach on a lattice, where sites with the same cell index form cells of variable shapes”. In a vertex model a cell is a polygon of vertices, and “a competition between cell–cell adhesive stresses and cell cortical tension control changes of cell shape”.

  • Phase-field models

    Phase-field models draw a cell as a diffuse interface coupled to actin flow. [10]

    More

    One example is a “phase field model that can couple actin flows with physical forces”.

Axis by axis

  • Solvent and hydrodynamic interaction

    Aleph

    One Stokes solvent for the whole world.approximationMore

    Nodes and surface faces couple to it as immersed boundaries, so a moving node carries its neighbours through the fluid. Drag on each node alone (6πηR, no fluid) remains a declared alternative; for anything run as a cell it is a labelled approximation.

    Engine scene (30 September, build 730e486a8, before the 7 October changes): two beads settling side by side.More

    With the Stokes solvent each bead carries the other and the pair settles at 1.088 × the one-bead Stokes speed; with drag on each node only it settles at 1.000 ×. This shows the difference in what is modelled; no closed form was scored for this scene.

    What the other tools' papers say · 2

    Cytosim. Fibres move with “the averaged mobility of a straight rod”. The paper notes: “An exact calculation would require finding the hydrodynamic interactions between all the points in the system. This can be done in the future.” [1]

    AFINES. Each bead's damping uses the Stokes relation; the paper states: “We have also ignored hydrodynamic interactions between filament beads.” [2]

  • Molecules: explicit or lumped

    Aleph

    Filaments, motor heads and cross-linkers are explicit nodes and relations.More

    Closed forms such as Bell–Evans or Hill are used only as predictions to check against, never as the law the engine runs.

    What the other tools' papers say · 1

    Cytosim, AFINES, MEDYAN. Also explicit-agent models: “Many agent-based models for the cytoskeleton exist, including publicly-available software Cytosim, AFINES, and MEDYAN.” MEDYAN's paper applies it to a network of “actin filaments, alpha-actinin cross-linking proteins, and non-muscle myosin IIA mini-filaments”. On this axis Aleph is not different in kind. [4] [3]

  • Filaments

    Aleph

    Filaments are chains of nodes: each segment carries an axial law (linear or stiffening) and each triple of nodes a bending law.More

    Near-inextensibility comes from the stiff axial law, which the linear solve treats exactly along each chain, and moves follow the filament's own arc. Exact length constraints and an augmented-Lagrangian length law are also in the engine. All of these are implemented. Each is now going through testing, and any that shows no advantage will be dropped.

    What the other tools' papers say · 3

    Cytosim. Fibres are “an equidistributed string of points”; “we use constraints in order to maintain the length of the fibers”, with bending elasticity. [1]

    AFINES. “We model actin filaments as polar wormlike chains (WLCs)”: bead chains with harmonic stretch and bending potentials. [2]

    MEDYAN. A “polymer model based on elongated cylindrical monomer segments for simulating semi-flexible polymers”, with bending through an angular potential. [3]

  • Rigid bodies (six degrees of freedom)

    Aleph

    A rigid body moves as one six-degree-of-freedom object while its nodes keep their mass and forces: motion is held to a centre plus a rotation, the linear solve runs on the body's six modes, and the line search follows the exact rigid path.More

    In the fluid mesh fitted to the nodes, a rigid body is a hole with no fluid grid inside. All of these are implemented. Each is now going through testing, and any that shows no advantage will be dropped.

    What the other tools' papers say · 2

    Cytosim. “a solid is a set of points that behaves like a solid body”. The source does not use six-degree-of-freedom wording. [1]

    AFINES, MEDYAN. The sources held here do not describe rigid bodies, so they are not compared.

  • Contact between filaments and surfaces

    Aleph

    Contact is part of every step: point–triangle and edge–edge barrier pairs and confinement to surfaces are terms of the same world energy, and before each line search they set the longest stride that keeps surfaces from passing through each other.

    What the other tools' papers say · 3

    AFINES. “we do not include steric interactions for our filaments, motors, and cross-linkers”. [2]

    MEDYAN. Cylinders exclude each other through a “novel approach for calculating excluded volume interactions”. [3]

    Cytosim. The general rule is not stated in the source held here; one example in it has “no steric interaction between the fibers”. [1]

  • Time integration and stop rule

    Aleph

    Each step is an implicit (backward-Euler) step solved by Newton iterations to the force residual tol_F with the relative rule above.FORCE-ACCEPTEDMore

    A step that stops at its iteration cap is kept and labelled FORCE-ACCEPTED.

    What the other tools' papers say · 4

    Cytosim. Motion is “described by a first-order multivariate Langevin equation”, integrated with “an implicit integration scheme”. [1]

    AFINES. “We use overdamped Langevin dynamics to solve for the motion of filament beads, motors, and cross-linkers.” [2]

    MEDYAN. The system is mechanically equilibrated “after a number of stochastic chemical reaction-diffusion steps”; “an energy minimization is performed using a conjugate gradient method”. [3]

    Stop criteria. The sources held here do not describe the other tools' stopping tolerances, so they are not compared.

  • General engine or cytoskeleton code

    Aleph

    A world is built only from declared data: nodes, relations, surfaces, rigid bodies and fluid regions.More

    Contact, area and permeation follow data tags, never an object's name, and a test blocks name-based branches. Membrane and nuclear envelope are deformable surfaces made of nodes.

    What the other tools' papers say · 2

    Cytosim. “Fibers, spheres and other voluminous objects are represented with points. Different types of connections are used to link the points together and in this way create composite mechanical structures.” [1]

    MEDYAN. Boundaries are “non-deformable shells” that “sterically repel approaching polymer segments”. [3]

  • Scale and population

    Aleph

    Aleph aims to draw conclusions only at the full native population of a cell (about 7.5 million nodes in the build of 22 September).More

    So far, development worlds from a few hundred to about 500,000 nodes have run. The one full-size run at 310 K (10 October) stopped after 6 steps, none at tol_F.

    Aleph

    Every physics kernel is NVIDIA Warp on a CUDA GPU.More

    There is no CPU simulation path, and a run that resolves a non-GPU device stops.
